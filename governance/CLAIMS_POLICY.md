# JExec Claims Policy

Version 1.0 · Applies to every public statement made under the JExec name.

## Rule

Every public claim must be **clear, bounded, verifiable, and versioned where
necessary**. In a category about trustworthy decisions, an inflated claim is
self-disqualifying. Credibility outranks speed, always.

## What JExec may currently be called

- A pre-execution admissibility **specification** (draft v0.1).
- A decision model, ontology (EAO), standard (JAS), protocol, and record format.
- A deterministic reference engine (JExec Check).

## What JExec must not be called (until verifiably true)

- A certification authority — there is no JAS certificate; no product may claim
  one, including ours.
- An enforcement engine, security scanner, or observability tool.
- A replacement for SLSA, Sigstore, or OPA. JExec defines the decision those
  systems inform. Positioning is always *between*, never *instead of*.
- An adopted industry standard. Adoption is claimed only when it exists and can
  be cited.

## Alignment language

Statements about the JExec Record aligning with in-toto-style attestations or
CloudEvents-style envelopes are **design directions**, not compatibility
claims, and must be phrased as such.

## Marketing constraint

No superlatives without a measurement. No "trusted by" without named, consenting
parties. No usage numbers without a public methodology. The words "governed",
"deterministic", and "append-only" may only describe things that verifiably are.

## Enforcement

A published claim found to violate this policy is a governance incident: it is
corrected publicly, and the correction is logged in `DECISION_LOG.md`.
